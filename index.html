What RECAST Does 1× speed

RECAST turns what a vision-language model reads from the scene, and any constraint the user states, into one Actionable Cost map: the VLM's judgments of ground safety, collision risk, goal direction, and passable gaps are grounded into geometry, and the robot chooses its path on that same map, so its reasoning and its motion cannot misalign.

TL;DR

  • Why. Robots in human spaces must read a complex scene and follow what their users ask. Policies learned from demonstrations cannot take such a request, and VLM-based planners rarely turn their reasoning into grounded motion.
  • Actionable Cost map (AC map). RECAST turns a VLM's reading of the scene into one cost map the robot acts on directly. From the frame, the goal, and the user's instruction, the VLM judges where the ground is safe to walk, which objects risk a collision, which way leads to the goal, and which gaps are passable.
  • Grounded, and refreshed every frame. Expert vision models pin each judgment to where it holds in the image. Between VLM answers the map is rebuilt on every frame, so it keeps up with a moving scene.
  • Navigation on the map. The same map conditions the trajectory decoder and scores every candidate path, so what the VLM judges is what the robot does, without retraining.

RECAST Pipeline

A VLM recasts the pivot frame (red) and the instruction into four judgments, which expert vision models ground into component cost maps and compose into the pivot AC map. Each current frame (green) rebuilds only the traversability and keep-out components without a new VLM call, and the decoder's candidate trajectories are scored on both maps before the best one is executed.
